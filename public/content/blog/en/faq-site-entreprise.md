Customers often ask the same questions before they call: how a project starts, whether you cover their area, or what information you need. A short FAQ can answer these questions at the right moment, provided it is based on real conversations.

## Collect questions from everyday enquiries

Look at emails, calls and meeting notes. Which questions come up repeatedly? Ask colleagues who speak with customers. Group similar questions together and keep the wording people actually use.

Do not create questions merely to repeat a keyword or praise your business. “How long does an initial appointment take?” is useful. “Why are we the best choice?” usually sounds like advertising.

## Answer directly, then add context

Lead with the answer. If timing or price varies, explain what it depends on rather than giving a misleading fixed figure. Link to a detailed service page when a subject needs more space.

Keep the FAQ small enough to scan. If a question becomes essential to deciding whether to use a service, answer it on the service page as well. Important information should not be hidden behind an accordion.

## Place questions where they help

A question about a particular service belongs near that service. General questions about contact, areas covered or the first conversation can sit on a main FAQ or contact page. On mobile, check that headings are easy to read and answers easy to open.

## Keep answers current

Review the FAQ when services, hours, prices or processes change. Remove answers that no longer match what your team does. A short, accurate FAQ builds more confidence than a long one that contradicts your contact page.

Its purpose is simple: help visitors make a decision and arrive at the first conversation with the right expectations.
