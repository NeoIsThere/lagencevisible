Google does not learn about your business in the same way as someone who walks into your premises. It builds an understanding from the information available online, how that information is organised and the links between it. Here are the main signals to be aware of.

## Pages provide context

Each page should cover a clearly defined topic. The homepage presents the business as a whole. A service page explains a particular service. The contact page confirms the business’s location and contact details.

This clear separation helps Google associate individual pages with relevant searches. It also allows visitors to land directly on the information they need.

## Titles introduce the topic

The page title shown in the browser tab and the main heading visible on the page signal its central topic. Descriptive wording is more useful than an abstract slogan.

Subheadings then guide readers through the content. They distinguish between the approach, areas covered, answers to common questions and ways to get in touch.

## Text provides the details

Google needs written content to understand services. Images alone are not enough. The wording should explain the business naturally, without artificially repeating the same phrase.

Useful content answers the questions customers are likely to ask: what need does the service address? How does it work? Where is it available? What happens next?

## Location clarifies the service area

The business address, town or city and areas served must be accurate. They can appear on the website, Google Business Profile and relevant directories. Keeping this information consistent avoids ambiguity.

There is little value in creating dozens of almost identical pages that change only the name of a town or city. An honest, precise description of the area covered is far more useful.

## Links connect information

Internal links show how your pages fit together. Links from the homepage to each important service help visitors and search engines find more detailed information.

Links from other websites can also improve discoverability and credibility when they are natural and relevant. Quality matters more than quantity.

## Structured data reduces ambiguity

Structured data can make it explicit that a page describes an organisation, a service, a person or an article. It supports the visible content but never replaces it.

A sitemap provides a list of important pages. Tools such as Google Search Console can then help monitor how those pages are crawled and identify potential problems.

## Google Business Profile complements the website

A local listing lets people quickly see opening hours, location and contact details. It should link to the official domain and remain consistent with it.

> The website provides detail; the local listing supports local discovery. They work better together.

None of these elements guarantees a particular ranking. Together, however, they provide a clear foundation that helps Google crawl, interpret and present the business in relevant searches.
