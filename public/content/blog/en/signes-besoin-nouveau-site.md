A website can remain live for years while gradually becoming less useful to the business. Age itself is not always the issue: problems arise when the website no longer reflects the business, suits the way people use mobile devices or meets customer expectations.

## You do not have a website yet

Having no website is the clearest sign. A local listing or social profile may be useful, but neither can present the full range of services or provide a lasting official presence.

Without a website, prospective customers trying to check your services find little reliable information and may continue their search elsewhere.

## The design feels outdated

An outdated website does not mean that the business does poor work. Visitors, however, can only judge what they see. An ageing interface, tiny text or distorted images can create doubt before they have even read about the services.

A redesign involves more than changing the colours. It should reorganise the main messages, simplify navigation and make the intended action easier.

## The website performs poorly on mobile

If visitors have to zoom in, scroll horizontally or aim for a button that is too small, the website is demanding unnecessary effort. Essential information should be immediately readable on a narrow screen.

Test your website on a real phone: can someone understand the business within a few seconds? Are the telephone number, form and directions easy to use?

## The information is incorrect

An out-of-date telephone number, incorrect address or service that is no longer available immediately undermines trust. These inconsistencies can also make it harder for search engines to understand the business.

A website should be easy to update. If every correction becomes risky or complicated, its technical foundations probably need to be reviewed.

## Your services are difficult to understand

A homepage cannot do everything. When several services are crammed into one vague paragraph, visitors cannot tell whether their needs are genuinely covered.

Dedicated service pages allow each topic to be explained in clear language, with relevant examples and a suitable call to action.

## Getting in touch takes too much effort

An overly long form, a buried button or an email address that is difficult to find can all lead to lost enquiries. The ideal journey remains simple: understand, feel reassured and then make contact.

> A new website becomes necessary when the old one creates more friction than trust.

Before rebuilding the website, identify which content remains useful and which questions your customers frequently ask. The new structure can then retain what already works while removing the obstacles.
