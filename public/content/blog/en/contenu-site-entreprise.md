An effective business website does not need dozens of pages. Above all, it should answer the questions people ask before getting in touch. These are the essentials it should cover.

## Introduce the business straight away

The opening section of the homepage should explain what the business does, who it helps and where it operates. Visitors should not have to decipher a slogan to work out whether they are in the right place.

Add a clear call to action: send an enquiry, ask a question or outline a need. One primary action is often enough.

## Pages for key services

A list of services gives an overview but rarely answers every question. Each key service deserves its own page, covering the need it addresses, what the process involves, its benefits and the information required to get started.

These pages also provide useful entry points from search results. People can arrive directly on the topic that interests them rather than having to find it on a general page.

## Verifiable information

Concrete details help people understand how the business operates. Explain its approach, qualifications and anything else that supports an informed choice. Use only accurate, verifiable information.

It is better to leave a detail out than to invent a customer, testimonial or result.

## An identifiable team

Names, genuine professional photographs and short biographies make the business feel more tangible. Explain who customers will speak to and what experience lies behind the work.

Avoid generic images that could belong to any team. Authenticity inspires more confidence than a perfectly staged scene.

## The service area

State clearly where the business operates: a town or city, a French department, a region or throughout France, depending on the reality. This helps visitors work out whether the business covers their area and gives search engines useful context.

Keep the contact details consistent across the contact page, local listing and professional profiles.

## Simple contact options

Keep the initial form short. Ask for a name, a way to reply and only the information needed to prepare for the first conversation. Further details can be collected later.

Also display an email address and, where relevant, a telephone number. Explain what happens after the form is submitted to reduce uncertainty.

## An FAQ based on real questions

A useful FAQ addresses common concerns: what content to provide, how the website works on mobile, who owns the website and how enquiries are handled. It should not be used simply to repeat keywords.

> Every page should help visitors understand, trust or take action.

Before publishing, read the website as if you knew nothing about the business. After a minute, could you explain what it offers? Would you know where it operates and how to get in touch? If so, the essentials are in place.
