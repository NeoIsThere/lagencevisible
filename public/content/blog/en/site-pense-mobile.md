Mobile is no longer a secondary way to access the web. For many visitors, a phone is the first—and sometimes the only—screen they use to find a business, check its services and get in touch.

## Searches often begin on a phone

Someone might search while travelling, out on a job, between appointments or at the exact moment a need arises. They expect a quick answer without having to sit down at a computer.

Your website should therefore show the essentials straight away: what you do, where you operate, the value you provide and how to contact you.

## Speed shapes the experience

Mobile connections can be less reliable. Heavy images, excessive animations and third-party tools can slow the page down and make the website appear broken.

A lean build, optimised images and loading only essential resources reduce the wait. A fast website feels easier to use and more trustworthy.

## Reading should require no effort

Tiny text and overlong lines are tiring to read. Headings should be clear, paragraphs short and contrast strong enough.

Buttons need a comfortably sized touch target and a clear label. ‘Request a website analysis’ is more informative than ‘Click here’. Complex menus should give way to short, predictable navigation.

## Contact should be immediate

Mobile visitors should be able to tap to call, send an email or open directions without copying details manually. An effective form asks only for the information needed for an initial response.

Consider the keyboard displayed for each field as well: an email field should open the appropriate keyboard, as should a telephone number field.

## A poor mobile experience damages trust

Visitors do not always see a layout fault as an isolated problem. If a button extends beyond the screen or a form cannot be submitted, they may simply decide that the business is difficult to contact.

> On mobile, every point of friction feels larger.

## Designing for mobile from the outset

Shrinking a large-screen design at the end often leads to content being hidden or squeezed. Starting with small screens forces you to prioritise what truly matters.

The result benefits every screen size: a clearer message, simpler navigation and more visible actions. At a minimum, test common screen widths, keyboard navigation, phone orientation and forms on real devices.
