A general “Services” page can introduce several offers, yet leave visitors wondering whether a particular service fits their needs. A page devoted to one service can answer that question when it stays concrete.

## Start with the customer's situation

Avoid opening with a broad statement about passion or excellence. Name the need you address, then describe the service. A renovation company, for example, may need to distinguish a small repair from a full project.

The first screen should make sense to someone who does not know your trade. Keep technical terms when they matter, but explain them in plain language.

## Show what the service includes

Outline the main steps: an initial conversation, assessment, proposal, delivery and follow-up, as appropriate to your work. Explain what depends on the situation instead of making a promise you cannot keep. If a fixed price is impossible, describe what affects the quote.

A short list makes the page easier to scan. Each item should answer a real question: what you deliver, what the customer should prepare or how the work takes place.

## Provide relevant evidence

An approved project photo, an explained method, a verifiable qualification or an anonymised example can make the offer tangible. Choose evidence related to the service on the page. A collection of logos or numbers without context can raise more questions than it answers.

## Make the next step clear

Once visitors understand the offer, they should know what to do. A link to your contact page may be enough. Its label should describe the real action: “Email us about this service” is clearer than “Discover more”.

Finally, read the page on a phone. The heading, steps and contact option should remain easy to find. If someone outside your business can summarise the service and explain how to reach you, the page is doing its job.
