A local business needs to be easy to place before a customer gets in touch. Visitors want to know what you do, where you work and how to reach you. Your website can answer those questions without promising a top position in search results.

## Describe the area you actually serve

State where your business is based and the towns or areas where you really work. If you cover several regions, explain any useful limits: visits by appointment, remote services or areas that depend on the service.

A long list of towns added only to attract searches rarely helps the reader. A concrete sentence such as “installation in Nice and nearby towns” is a more reliable guide. Tailor it to what your business actually offers.

## Connect each service to useful information

A service page should explain what you offer, who it is for and how an enquiry works. If availability varies by area, say so. A clear heading and contact details consistent with your other channels help people recognise your business.

You can show local work if you have permission to publish it. Describe the problem and your part in solving it, without inventing testimonials or exposing private information.

## Keep your website and business listing consistent

If you use a Google Business Profile, check that the name, website, hours and contact details are correct. Your site can provide the detail a listing cannot always show: your approach, services, team and answers to common questions.

The listing and site play complementary roles. One helps people find a business; the other helps them decide whether it is right for them. For eligible businesses, [Google sets out the rules for Business Profiles](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177).

## Test the journey on a phone

Try the site on a phone: can you find the service area in seconds? Do the phone number, address and contact link work? Are the hours current? Ask someone unfamiliar with your business to repeat the journey.

Local visibility starts with accurate information that people can use. Correcting an address or clarifying a service area may help more than adding another vague page.
