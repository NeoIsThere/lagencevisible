A business can be well known locally and recommended by its customers while remaining almost absent online. This is not merely a visual issue: without an official source, search engines and AI assistants have little reliable information on which to base a description.

## The way people search has changed

Before getting in touch with a business, most people check what they can find online. They look for its name, services, location, opening hours or signs that it is trustworthy. They do this on Google and through assistants such as ChatGPT, Copilot and Gemini.

Search queries are no longer limited to two or three words. They can take the form of a complete question: ‘Which business near me can help with what I need?’ People expect a clear answer that takes their circumstances into account.

If your business does not have a website, a prospective customer may find an incomplete listing, an outdated directory entry or a rarely updated social profile. They then have to piece together what you offer. Often, they simply choose the business that makes things easier for them.

## Where do AI assistants find their information?

AI assistants draw on information available online, depending on how they work and which sources they can access. They look for a consistent picture built from the business name, what it does, its services, its location and content that answers specific questions.

A website cannot guarantee that an AI assistant will mention a business. What it can provide is an official, stable and structured source. Without one, the assistant must rely on external information that may be limited, outdated or contradictory.

> An assistant cannot properly understand a business that is not clearly explained anywhere.

## Why an official website matters

Your website is the online space you control. You decide what to say, how to order the information and which actions to offer visitors. The homepage sets out your value, service pages provide detail and the contact page confirms your details.

A good website can:

- present your business accurately;
- give each important service its own page;
- state where you operate;
- answer recurring questions;
- connect your other online profiles to one central source;
- make it easy to send an enquiry from a phone.

It also gives search engines a clear structure to crawl: descriptive headings, internal links, descriptions, structured data and a sitemap.

## What happens when information is missing

A missed enquiry leaves no trace. Someone who cannot find your website will not write to explain what made them hesitate. They return to the search results, open another website and approach a competitor instead.

The sequence is simple: a search, missing information, doubt and the choice of another business. The more a decision depends on trust, the more important an official, carefully presented online presence becomes.

## Where should you begin?

Start with the real questions prospective customers ask: who are you, what do you offer, where do you operate and how can they contact you? These answers form the foundation of your website.

Then add pages for key services, any genuine supporting evidence, a useful FAQ and the technical elements required for crawling. The aim is not to publish as much as possible, but to provide information that is accurate, original and easy to understand.
