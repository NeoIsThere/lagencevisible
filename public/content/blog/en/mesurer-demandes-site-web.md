A website can attract visits without bringing in relevant enquiries. To understand whether it helps your business, follow the whole journey: being found, being understood and being contacted.

## Define a useful enquiry

Not every email has the same value. Decide which enquiries match the services and areas you cover. Record their number, subject and outcome without collecting more personal information than you need.

For a small business, a simple spreadsheet may be enough. The aim is not a large report; it is to see whether the right people find answers on your site.

## Separate search visibility from contact

Google Search Console reports impressions, clicks and pages shown in Google Search, among other things. Those figures describe search visibility; on their own they do not count emails or phone calls received. [Google explains the Search Console reports](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-console-start).

You can compare those figures with enquiries actually received, while remembering that someone may discover your business through several channels. Do not automatically credit every contact to the last page they viewed.

## Look for obstacles

If a page attracts visitors but few enquiries, read it again: is the service clear? Is the area covered stated? Does the contact button explain what will happen? Does the link work on a phone?

Ask customers too: what helped them choose you, and what information was missing? Their answers may reveal a problem that numbers cannot show.

## Compare over a sensible period

One week can be misleading, particularly for seasonal businesses. Compare similar periods and note major changes to the website or business. Where possible, change one thing at a time and then observe the next enquiries.

The right measure depends on your goal. An agency website that receives a small number of well-matched messages can be more useful than a heavily visited site that produces no relevant conversation.
